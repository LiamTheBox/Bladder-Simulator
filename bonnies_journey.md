# Bonnie's Long Journey

The glowing red digits on the nightstand clicked to 4:10 AM. Under the blanket, twenty-three-year-old Bonnie curled her toes and bit her lip. She hadn't used the restroom since yesterday afternoon, and the pressure was becoming entirely unreasonable. 

Today was departure day. A twenty-hour international journey lay ahead, and Bonnie had a personal, stubborn rule when it came to travel: flawless manners, absolute composure, and never, ever inconveniencing anyone. It was a bizarre endurance challenge she’d set for herself, a test of pure willpower.

She slid out of bed slowly, knowing every spring in her childhood mattress was a tattletale. Her older sister, Chloe, was asleep in the next room. Bonnie crept down the fourteen stairs, navigating the creaky fourth and seventh steps from years of muscle memory. She reached the downstairs powder room, closed the door, and finally allowed herself absolute relief. The clock was reset. The real journey began now.

By 8:00 AM, the house was alive with the chaotic energy of a family seeing their daughter off. Chloe, fully dressed and buzzing with espresso, helped Bonnie lace up the back of her stylish, albeit restrictive, travel bodysuit. 

"You look amazing," Chloe said, pulling the laces tight. "Like a superhero."

Downstairs, their mother had prepared a massive breakfast spread. "Drink up, sweetheart," she beamed, pouring a massive, steaming mug of green tea. "You have such a long flight. Hydration is everything!"

Bonnie smiled, her people-pleasing instincts taking over. She couldn't refuse her mother on departure day. She drank the entire mug, feeling the warm, heavy liquid settle into her stomach.

The airport at 10:00 AM was a test of patience. The security queue snaked endlessly. As Bonnie approached the scanner, the TSA officer pointed to the oversized iced tea she had foolishly bought at the terminal café. "No liquids past this point, miss. Drink it or bin it."

Not wanting to waste a perfectly good eight-dollar drink, Bonnie smiled politely, tipped her head back, and chugged the entire thing. The officer looked mildly impressed. Bonnie’s bladder, however, filed a formal complaint. 

By 1:00 PM, she was locked into seat 34A for a fourteen-hour flight. Her kingdom consisted of a few square feet of window seat, walled in by a large, deeply asleep businessman in the aisle seat. 

Hours ticked by. Turbulence bounced the cabin, the seatbelt digging squarely into her lower abdomen. When the flight attendant arrived with the meal cart, she offered a radiant smile. "Tea, coffee, or juice?" 

"Just water, please," Bonnie asked softly. 

"I'll give you the large one," the attendant whispered kindly, handing over a massive plastic cup. Bonnie drank it, maintaining her pleasant, unbothered facade even as every jolt of the aircraft sent shockwaves through her core. The businessman never woke up, and Bonnie refused to wake him to ask to pass. She would endure.

Landing at 8:30 PM local time was a relief, but the pressure was now critical. She breezed through immigration with a tight, rehearsed smile, hoisted her fifty-pound suitcase off the baggage carousel with a sharp exhale, and walked out to the arrivals hall.

"Bonnie!" 

Her host sister, a twenty-three-year-old student named Irina, sprinted over alongside her mother, Mrs. Higgins. Mrs. Higgins pulled Bonnie into a fierce, suffocating hug that squeezed her middle like a stress toy. Bonnie’s eyes watered, which Mrs. Higgins warmly interpreted as sheer joy. 

"Want to use the restroom before we drive, love?" Mrs. Higgins offered, jingling her car keys. 

"I'm perfectly fine!" Bonnie lied brightly, her voice jumping half an octave. 

The car ride was an agonizing forty minutes of coastal roundabouts. When they finally arrived at the Higgins home, Mrs. Higgins immediately poured a massive, welcoming pot of tea. Bonnie, a portrait of the perfect guest, accepted a cup. 

At 11:30 PM, the day caught up with her. She was finally in her guest room, the restrictive bodysuit unlaced, but there was a catch. Irina had casually mentioned the only working bathroom upstairs was the en-suite attached to Irina’s own room. "Just come through whenever! I sleep like the dead," Irina had promised.

Except, Irina didn't sleep. She stayed awake playing music and talking through the wall until well past 2:00 AM. Bonnie paced her room in tiny circles, her hands pressed against her stomach. She had held it for over twenty-two hours. She was a marvel of human engineering, but the dam was about to burst. 

She weighed her options: sneak through a stranger's dark bedroom in the middle of the night, or hold out just a little longer. Irina had mentioned she always went for a run at dawn. 

Bonnie sat on the edge of her bed, breathed slowly, and waited.

At exactly 6:58 AM, the front door downstairs clicked shut. Footsteps crunched on the gravel outside, fading into the distance. 

The house was empty. 

Bonnie stood up on shaking legs. She walked out of her room, through the hallway, and into the unguarded en-suite. She locked the door behind her, leaned against the vanity, and finally let go. It was the most colossal, silent, and hard-earned relief of her life. 

She washed her hands, looked at her tired, victorious reflection in the mirror, and smiled. The perfect record stood.
